# Import a PV*SOL project

From **Open**, choose a `.pvprj` and a location for a new `.solarcraft` copy. The source archive is read locally. On Windows, choose a destination folder. SolarCraft uses the original basename and adds a suffix if the name is already taken. The CLI call `import_pvsol` takes `source`. Omit `file` to preview. To create a project, supply a new `.solarcraft` file. In MCP these paths are relative to the workspace.

The importer accepts the observed ZIP formats 15, 20, 23 and 24 with embedded `Project.xml`, `DB.json`, and a recognized three-channel WBV. It pins equipment revisions from the archive, imports module areas and their saved orientation, and reads every wiring block. Saved MPPT assignments determine active module counts. A genuinely unwired area remains available for editing and contributes zero generation.

- **Series and optimizers.** Series-connected segments share one current. Supported per-module buck optimizers use their saved voltage, current and power limits and efficiency curve. The desktop wiring editors display these groups and preserve them when applying other changes. Advanced groups can be edited through `set_stringing`. Enter verified DC cable ratings manually for these groups.
- **Active tracking.** Saved north–south and east–west designs use one moving axis. Vertical trackers rotate the saved tilt, and dual-axis trackers follow the sun within both rotation limits. SolarCraft evaluates orientation, incidence and diffuse optics every hour. The Design page can edit each array’s tracking mode, axis and travel, and the ground coverage ratio for backtracking. The single-axis tilt slopes down in its compass azimuth. Archived comparison data provide system-level energy. Per-array tracker angles remain unverified.
- **Saved and edited shading.** The importer checks each physical inverter, MPPT, string shape and module slot. Supported ordinary and cross-area series strings use per-module I–V curves with full-cell or half-cell bypass groups. Supported buck optimizers use the shaded module’s curve within the archived voltage, current, power and efficiency limits. The same solver accepts substring shadows from bound 3D fields or obstacle roofs after edits. For native scenes, assign an array’s routes in the Scene inspector or send a `bind_string_routes` scene edit with `array` and `bindings`. Each binding supplies `route` and `binding: {unit_id, input, group, copy}`, with zero-based indices. A nonempty assignment must cover the whole array. An empty list restores inference by route length and order. Generic scene exchange requires destination-project assignments.
- **Editable 3D scenes.** The observed DirectX mesh and drawn roofs restore editable objects, panels and routes. The importer supports rectangular shed, gable and hip shells, attached shed and gabled dormers, and upright trees. Two Dormers restores 36 modules across its two parallel roofs. Translations carry attached roof modules and preserve their string routes. Re-place panels after changing the supporting roof’s pitch or shape. The shells omit overhang surfaces, roof cutouts, openings, slab thickness and textures. Object-only scenes retain archived shade. With bound module placements a committed edit switches to SolarCraft’s geometry.
- **Rear irradiance.** Applicable saved hourly or monthly rear profiles are used by default. Without one, bound finite ground racks over a horizontal datum use scene rays for rear beam, sky visibility and single-bounce ground reflection. The model includes mutual row shadows, finite edges and visible scene obstructions, and applies the array-average rear irradiance to the module curves. When finite rack geometry is unavailable, saved ViewFactor pitch, width, clearance and mounting shade support the periodic-row estimate. The bifacial settings in Design or `force_geometry: true` under `/arrays/N/bifacial_rows` force an independent estimate. Results include monthly rear irradiation and the source.
- **PhotoPlan.** Supported four-line and rectangle records restore the original source photograph, calibration, a rectified view, the roof outline and saved module placements. The importer checks its projected outline against the archived image-space polygon before attaching the layout. Use the Roof editor to inspect or modify it. Other calibration types, multi-plane files and obstacle records still require migration.
- **Legacy island designs.** An identical set of battery clusters on one AC bus becomes an equivalent battery bank. SolarCraft imports the battery and converter ratings, the generator where selected, and available appliance load intervals. It reconstructs supported appliance and generated-load shapes on its reference calendar. Import warnings describe the assumptions for initial SOC, battery dispatch, generator switching and load shedding.
- **Grid designs.** SolarCraft imports a recognized single embedded load profile at its original interval, or the hourly base-load ledger when available. A feed-in limit relative to the DC power becomes the corresponding kW limit; an absolute limit is stored by PV*SOL in the same field in units of 100 kW (0.025 → 2.5 kW) and is imported as such. Review the import warnings for settings that require separate migration.
- **Grid-connected battery systems.** The saved system type determines whether storage is enabled. An embedded AC battery system uses its integrated converter, including when the saved DC flag conflicts with the catalog. A generator-side or DC-link (catalog coupling 3) battery with a matching inverter assignment uses that inverter's hybrid port. SolarCraft imports the embedded cells and the converter's nominal power, charge and discharge limits and E0…E100 part-load curve. The detailed battery starts fully charged and uses self-consumption dispatch. Published `InternalResistance` is converted from the catalog battery block to a single cell using its series and parallel counts. Unsupported configurations keep the editable PV design and show an import warning. DC-link systems, multiple systems, timed dispatch and boost, float and equalisation stages still require migration.
- **Loss settings.** Albedo, soiling and manufacturer mismatch come from the PV areas. PV*SOL's loss chain has no light-induced degradation step, so the import sets SolarCraft's LID to zero, and DC wiring loss is zero when the project has no DC cable lengths.
- **Module model.** When every imported module is flagged for standard part-load operation, the project uses the two-diode model with PV*SOL's preferred standard-parameter fit (`two_diode_pvsol`): cell topology from Voc / 0.6 V unless the declared cell count is within four, idealities 1 and 2, the third series resistance on a 0.1 mΩ grid that gives a valid recombination current, and a shunt resistance that rises at low irradiance. A half-cell module whose first topology fails is retried with half its declared cells in series. A module the fit still rejects falls back to the one-diode model, as in PV*SOL. Other modules keep SolarCraft's one-diode model.
- **Module temperature.** When `SimResults.xml` holds one monthly module temperature series, SolarCraft simulates the imported design once and scales its temperature coefficients so its all-hours monthly mean rise above ambient matches the archived one (the Faiman rise is G / (U0 + U1·v), so one factor scales it exactly). The import warning states the factor.

The AC channel uses PV inverter output for a design with a separate AC battery converter or no battery. For an imported generator-side DC hybrid that still uses hybrid coupling, SolarCraft compares delivery by PV and battery to the load, grid and feed-in limiter. The DC channel uses array output before MPP matching. SolarCraft counts MPP matching in the inverter step and PV*SOL lists it as a DC loss.

Run the imported design and open **Results → Saved PV*SOL comparison**, or call `compare_pvsol` with the imported `file`. The saved reference and the SolarCraft run are separate. SolarCraft compares annual energy and whatever monthly or 8,760-row reference channels the file provides. The results show the signed kWh and percentage difference, mean absolute and root-mean-square hourly error, and whether the current run uses the imported input hash.

| Reference | Use |
| --- | --- |
| SimResults.xml | First project-total PV DC and inverter AC groups. Annual and monthly energy are stored in watt-seconds. An empty model marker means no qualifying saved aggregate run. |
| core.results.3600 | Recognized hourly PV DC and inverter AC channels. The time column ends each consecutive local-standard-time interval. Signed AC includes standby in the archived series. |
| OecForeCast.json | The signed hourly PV generation ledger is the AC reference when the simulator's own hourly export is absent. Available hourly series supply missing annual and monthly totals. |
| WBV | Embedded temperature, global horizontal radiation and wind. Row *k* is centred on hour mark *k*:00 local standard time (its daily irradiance profile is symmetric about solar noon there); SolarCraft evaluates the sun at that mark and keeps the row in calendar hour *k* of a 365-day local standard-time reference year. It applies Erbs decomposition when saved diffuse readings are absent. With saved DHI it reverses the source spectral correction before deriving DNI with the midpoint solar position and limits nonphysical values near the horizon. |

The comparison covers PV generation. Open areas include unsupported multi-plane layouts, seasonal geometry shading, vendor-specific optimizer control, reflected light from sloped terrain and roofs, module-specific rear mismatch, unsupported photo layouts and storage topologies, EV and thermal controls, adaptive time steps, tariff details, and equipment protection. SolarCraft applies its normal electrical validation to imported designs. If validation blocks a run, the saved annual totals remain available in the reference.
