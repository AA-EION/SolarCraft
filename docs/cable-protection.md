# Cable protection checks

Select a rule set per circuit. The circuit editor shows each measured or entered value with its comparison threshold. The Planner report includes the result. Missing ratings remain **Incomplete** and keep the affected circuit from being applied to the simulation.

## NEC 2023 PV DC

The ordinary method for PV DC source/output circuits uses 1.25 times the parallel STC short-circuit current for the maximum circuit current. The unadjusted conductor ampacity and the minimum protective-device current use another factor of 1.25. The corrected ampacity must carry the maximum circuit current. A linked MPPT gets its STC current from the module and string design. When one cable runs to each string, sizing uses the highest string current. For an unlinked run, supply the STC current per conductor path.

Enter the insulation-table ampacity, installation correction factor, and terminal-limited ampacity separately. The tool also checks the entered equipment limit, maximum DC circuit voltage, device voltage rating and fault interruption rating. It applies the ordinary method from NEC 690.8/690.9 with voltage and interruption checks. The checks use the exact entered device size, with no next-standard-size, 100%-rated assembly or alternative maximum-current exception. [NFPA 70](https://www.nfpa.org/codes-and-standards/nfpa-70-standard-development/70) · [University of Maryland Extension](https://www.extension.umd.edu/resource/working-solar-wiring-and-fusing-eb-2023-0676)

## IEC 60364-4-43

The overload checks use Ib ≤ In ≤ Iz and I₂ ≤ 1.45 Iz. The short-circuit checks compare the device's breaking capacity to the available fault current and its let-through energy to (k × conductor area)² in the adiabatic range. Enter I₂, conductor k for the chosen insulation and material, and let-through energy from the device data at the relevant fault duty. The checks also compare the entered circuit and device voltage and AC/DC rating. [IEC 60364-4-43:2023](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/28432) · [Schneider Electric overload checks](https://www.electrical-installation.org/enwiki/Practical_values_for_a_protective_scheme) · [Schneider Electric short-circuit withstand](https://www.electrical-installation.org/enwiki/Verification_of_the_withstand_capabilities_of_cables_under_short-circuit_conditions)

## Radial AC fault duty and coordination

The fault study computes symmetrical RMS bolted current for a strong source at the device and a weak source at the remote end of one radial circuit. Enter resistance and reactance at the device for each selected fault type. A three-phase fault uses per-phase source impedance and phase voltage. Line–line and line–neutral or line–earth cases use the corresponding full source loop. The remote case adds outbound and return resistance at the entered temperature and the entered full-path reactance. Supply the neutral or PE size where applicable. The interrupting check uses the larger of the calculated near duty and any separately entered prospective duty. [Schneider Electric, earth-fault current](https://www.electrical-installation.org/enwiki/TN_system_-_Earth-fault_current_calculation)

For the device pair, enter the upstream **minimum trip or pre-arcing** curve and downstream **maximum total clearing** curve in absolute amperes and seconds. Enter points in increasing current order and use the manufacturer's published limits for comparison. The tool interpolates in log-log space and checks the time ratio at the endpoints and every curve breakpoint. It checks remote clearing against the entered time limit and, where the conductor factor k is supplied, the adiabatic limit up to five seconds. For the current-limiting region, a published selectivity limit for the exact pair and voltage can fill a gap in the curve coverage. Missing coverage is reported as incomplete. A documented curve conflict remains a failure. [Schneider Electric, device coordination](https://www.electrical-installation.org/enwiki/Coordination_between_circuit-breakers)

## Local rules

Colombia is the first local profile. Select the appropriate AC path from RETIE Libro 3 of Resolución 40117 de 2024. The tool checks 3% on an inverter-to-connection path under Article 3.17.21(d), 3% on a service at its disconnect under Title 26(f), and 5% on a feeder/branch path at design load under Title 26(f). The overhead service option also checks the stated minimum 8 AWG copper or 6 AWG aluminium, certification for outdoor service, and the AA-8000 grade for aluminium under Title 26(d). The project-authority option takes a documented limit and reference for other projects. [MinEnergía, RETIE Libro 3](https://www.minenergia.gov.co/documents/11566/4._Libro_3_-_Instalaciones.pdf) · [Current official RETIE register](https://minenergia.gov.co/es/misional/energia-electrica-2/reglamentos-tecnicos/reglamento-t%C3%A9cnico-de-instalaciones-el%C3%A9ctricas-retie/)

Enter the drop in the other portions of the path, or zero when the selected circuit is the whole path. The check uses the modeled resistive drop as a floor. Supply the reviewed segment drop when reactance or other installation conditions affect it. Grounding design and utility-specific exceptions fall outside these circuit checks.

## AC and DC network studies

Open **Cables → Network fault studies → Edit studies**. Use **Load synthetic example** to see the input format, then replace the example values with the project's impedances and manufacturer data. Preview calculates without saving. Saving replaces the study list as one undoable project edit, and the System Planner report shows the selected fault locations, device duties, and coordination. These are independent studies. Changes to stringing, cable sizes, or equipment do not update the entered network, source, and device data.

Each study models one radial operating configuration. Enter 2–64 unique buses as a single tree, one or more sources, and the bus IDs to short. Use a separate named study for configurations such as grid-connected and islanded operation. The CLI method is `network_faults`; call it with a project `file` to load the saved studies and example, `studies` to preview changes, or `studies` plus `"mode":"save"` to persist the list. The MCP tool uses the same inputs and a Boolean `save` flag with a workspace-relative file.

| Input | Units and interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| `domain`, `maximum_voltage_v` | `ac_three_phase` uses balanced three-phase fault current with line-to-line RMS voltage as input. `ac_loop` represents one explicit AC loop, such as an L–L or L–PE fault, with the appropriate loop voltage and total loop impedance. `dc_pole_to_pole` uses DC volts and the full outgoing and return resistance. |
| `links[].minimum_duty`, `links[].maximum_duty` | Resistance and reactance in mΩ per phase or per loop according to the domain. DC uses resistance. Include warm and cold conductor conditions in the respective scenarios. `from` and `to` are the installed forward direction of any attached device. |
| `sources[].minimum`, `sources[].maximum` | A `voltage` source uses the open-circuit volts and source impedance in mΩ. AC voltage sources use a common zero phase angle. A `current_limited` source uses documented fault amperes, AC phase angle in degrees, terminal-voltage bounds and time in seconds for which the contribution is supported. AC amperes are symmetrical RMS. Omit a source state for a disconnected source. Choose cases that bound duty at **each device**, including reverse-feed and source-out conditions. |
| `links[].device` | Enter the applicable `rating` (`ac` or `dc`), rated volts, interruption in kA, and closing withstand in peak kA. For a non-switching fuse, enter the relevant evaluated assembly's peak withstand where documented. For DC, use the device's published polarity, pole and time-constant configuration when determining the ratings. A `documented_peak_ka` value is a study-specific upper bound **at those device terminals**, supported by `peak_reference`. Set `reverse_rated` only when the device's installation rating permits bidirectional interruption. |
| Time-current curves | Curves use points in absolute A and s. The minimum operating band is minimum trip or fuse pre-arcing. The maximum clearing band includes all arc-clearing time. Enter the manufacturer's direct-comparison cutoff in kA. Curves are interpolated log-log without extrapolation. |

The solver collapses the radial branches into Norton equivalents relative to the shorted bus, then calculates currents at the sources and each device. It checks interruption, voltage rating, reverse duty, and maximum clearing time against the entered limit and the shortest specified current-limited support. For a single AC voltage source it derives prospective first-peak duty from the equivalent loop R/X using the IEC 60909-0:2016 peak factor. For DC, current-limited AC and multiple contributing AC sources, it requires a documented terminal peak bound and flags missing coverage. [IEC 60909-0:2016](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/24100) · [IEC 61660-1:1997, scope of DC auxiliary studies](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/5700)

For each modeled fault, the closest device is the primary on each source path. Its latest clearing time is compared with each upstream device's earliest operating time at that upstream device's own branch current. A conservative ratio can pass the comparison. A shortfall in a supported same-state comparison fails it. Overlap of separate bounds alone, unsupported source ranges, missing curves, or data outside the direct-comparison range are incomplete. A fault with separate primary devices requires separate review of the remaining feeds after the first device operates.

AC currents combine as phasors. Reports show their magnitudes at the fault, at each source terminal and through each device. For example, two AC sources with different fault-current phase angles may produce a total whose magnitude is smaller than the sum of their individual magnitudes.

This study covers bolted faults and steady duty envelopes on the entered radial topology. Meshed networks, inter-voltage coupling, machine transients, post-trip dynamic opening sequences, DC capacitive and inductive transients, arc flash, and current-limiting interactions require separate studies. An entered terminal peak bound remains an input with its own provenance.
